## Current state

**Playable?** Yes — and as of Session 9 it is a full run rather than a single screen. A monster walks a street of buildings, climbs their faces, walks their roofs, punches sections apart, and brings buildings down on top of itself if it doesn't jump clear. Punching open a wall section reveals whatever's behind it — food heals, bad items and live electrical hurt, wall dynamite has a fuse, a photographer or bathtub bather will knock the monster off if not eaten fast enough, an environmental weapon (manhole cover/flower pot/safe) can be knocked loose to fall on a vehicle or helicopter below, and sometimes a civilian or a monster's own designated victim instead of an item. Eating any civilian heals and scores; grabbing your own designated victim holds them (and scores the 4,000-6,000 hold bonus on the grab) until they struggle free, after which they're just another civilian to eat. Civilians also flee across the street on their own. National Guardsmen pop out of windows to shoot and lob dynamite (eating one removes the threat, heals, and scores) — unless the monster they're aiming at is holding its designated victim, in which case they hold fire (a courtesy that, per the single documented source, applies only to Guardsmen — every other military unit below keeps firing regardless). A second Guardsman variant plants a demolition charge at a building's base — the same route the helicopter's bombs use to bring a building down without paying the player a dime. A helicopter strafes or bombs. A tank and a police car now drive the street too, firing heavy shells that hit hard and knock the monster back far enough to clear a building's footprint — including off it entirely, the whole point of "large knockback." A paratrooper can drop onto a roof and defend that one building, rapid-firing only while a monster is actually on it. A lightning cloud drifts over and zaps whatever's under it when it fires — it's the one enemy that can't be punched down, a hazard rather than a unit, and has no scoring row either. Spawn cadence, concurrent-unit caps, the tank/police mix and the helicopter strafe/bomb split all scale off one difficulty scalar, which Session 9 wired to the real day index (`CONFIG.difficulty.scalar` survives as a manual debug offset). Energy hitting zero reverts the monster to a human placeholder that walks off screen.

As of Session 10 it is also a *social* game rather than a solo one. **Two monsters share the screen and the camera from the character select onward**, each on its own key set, with its own energy bar, its own score column and its own designated victim — and a third can buy in at any time, mid-day, on its own keys. The two of them are not merely co-existing: a punch that connects with another player's monster **drains that monster's bar for real**, and **knocks whatever it was holding out of its hands** onto the street, where either player can eat it. Every item, civilian and victim on the board is now a race — whoever's fist lands first gets it, and the loser's punch finds an empty window. And when a monster's bar finally empties, its human form walking off screen is no longer just that player's continue window: **the other player can eat it**, for points and a chunk of health, which ends the window on the spot and takes that player out of the run. A monster standing between you and a wall now absorbs the punch meant for the wall — but a civilian standing between the two of you still gets eaten first, deliberately, so the thing you're both racing for never becomes unreachable exactly when you're racing for it.

It is now a *game* rather than a sandbox: the street is a **day**, one of a data-driven list of cities. Levelling every building on the board ends the day, a newspaper-headline interstitial reports it, and the next city loads from data — building count, heights, palettes, level features, which slice of the military roster shows up, and how hard it all is. The list runs 128 days six times over (768 total, then back to day 1), difficulty ramps across the days of a pass and again with each pass, and clearing the 128th day of any pass fires the **mega-vitamin**: a full heal plus 5,000 points for everyone still in the run. Health now carries between days, which is what makes that reward mean anything. Some days have set-pieces: a **riverway** that drains health while you wade through it, a **bridge** that is the dry way across, a **pier**, a **boater** drifting on the water worth 750, and a **train** worth 500 every time you hit it and three hits to wreck. When a monster's energy hits zero it still reverts to a human and walks off — but now a **continue prompt** counts down that walk-off, and pressing that player's own punch/jump key buys back in at full health with the score intact. Let the window close and that player is out for the rest of the run while everyone else carries on; once every player's window has closed, the run ends.

Every point award — punching (25/225), a building coming down under its own monster (2,500, and *only* that monster), eating a civilian (500) or a Photographer before its flash (750), a food item (175 flat), grabbing a designated victim (4,000-6,000), eating a Guardsman/Paratrooper (50) or a Helicopter/Police car (750) or a Tank (200), and a dropped manhole cover/flower pot (500) or safe (100-500) connecting with a vehicle or helicopter — now lands on the right player's score and shows up live in a real three-column HUD (name, score, energy bar) that reserves empty columns for unjoined players rather than collapsing them away. Session 9 added three more reachable rows — the train (500 per hit), the boater (750) and the mega-vitamin bonus (5,000). Six §6 rows still have no code path because Phase 1 never built the thing they score: the three car states, the loot bag, and the "off" light-bulb/TV/neon variant. All six sit in `CONFIG.scoring` with the exact documented value, ready for whichever future session adds the entity.

**How to run:** open `index.html`. Press any player's start key at the title → each player picks a monster with left/right and confirms with punch or jump → play.
**P1 controls:** A/D move · **W** climb up / grab a face · **S** climb down / leave a roof · **G** jump · **F** punch (start: F/G/Enter).
**P2 controls:** arrows move/climb · **`/`** jump · **`.`** punch (start: `.` or `/`).
**P3 controls:** J/L move · I/K climb · **P** jump · **O** punch (start: O or P) — not in the run at startup; press **O** at the character select or *at any point during a day* to join in progress.
Jump-to-grab: jump toward a building and hold up in mid-air.
`CONFIG.players.activeAtStart` is **2**. Setting it to 3 is the whole change needed to open a run three-handed — nothing else in the code counts players (verified; see Verification — Session 10).
**Debug day jumping** (needs `CONFIG.debug.enabled`): **`]`** next day · **`[`** previous day · **`\`** jump to the next 128-day milestone. `CONFIG.debug.startDay` picks the day a fresh run opens on, and `RAMPAGE.jumpToDay(n)` does the same from the console. All three route through the one `Game.jumpToDay`.

## What changed last session

Session 10 — local multiplayer and monster-vs-monster, all inside `index.html`:

- **`CONFIG.players.activeAtStart: 1 → 2`** (§8.1) — the *only* number that decides how many players a run opens with. Everything Sessions 1-9 built for "up to 3" turned out to hold: the input maps, the player records, the 3-column HUD, per-player scoring, per-player continue windows and the mega-vitamin's per-player payout all worked at 2 and at 3 without a code change. That claim is tested rather than asserted — the suite sets `activeAtStart = 3` at runtime and plays a three-handed run end to end (see below).
- **`Monster.interactWithMonster` + `Game.monsterTargetFor`** (§8.2) — one method and one spatial query cover all three documented monster-vs-monster interactions, because which one fires is decided by nothing more than whether the target is already defeated. A live rival takes `CONFIG.health.damage.monsterPunch` (8 — the placeholder Session 4 left named and unused) through the ordinary `applyDamage` funnel, and **drops whatever it is holding**. A defeated rival's human form is **eaten** for `CONFIG.scoring.eatDefeatedMonster` (500) and `CONFIG.health.cannibalHeal` (25). No new stance, no new input, no separate cannibalize action.
- **The drop reuses `Human.struggleFree()` rather than adding a second release path** — so a punched-out victim is indistinguishable from one whose hold timer expired (Session 5's #29): it lands on the street, flees, and latches `grabbable = false`, meaning it can be eaten by either player and re-grabbed by neither. The holder keeps the §6 hold bonus, which Session 8 already decided is paid at the moment of the grab (#41). **[INFERRED — `13-inferred-items.md #8`]**, which is the one the session brief specifically asked to flag: §8.2 marks "punching a monster can make it drop what it's holding" as unconfirmed and instructs implementing drop-on-hit as the faithful-feeling default. Deliberately *not* added alongside it: knockback (§5.1 gives that to the tank/police shell specifically) and punch-animation interruption.
- **Cannibalism is matched against the figure that's actually drawn** — `Monster.humanFormRect()`, and `CONFIG.art.humanFormWidthFrac/HeightFrac` promoted out of `Monster.drawHuman`'s hardcoded `0.45`/`0.55`. They stopped being art-only the moment a punch had to hit them; a walk-off would otherwise have been a monster-sized target that looks half that big.
- **Eating the human form ends that player's continue window immediately** — and that consequence surfaced a genuine single-player assumption in Session 9's code. `updateContinueWindows` tested for "this player *was* awaiting a continue and its monster is now gone", which could only ever mean a finished walk-off, because a finished walk-off was the only way a monster could leave `PLAY`. Cannibalism adds a second way in, including on the very tick the human form appears — before any window was opened — which would have stranded that player in the run with nothing on the street and no way to end it. Rewritten to branch on **"is there a monster at all"**, which subsumes both routes; the old branch became unreachable and was deleted rather than left sitting there. **[INFERRED — `13-inferred-items.md #48`.]**
- **The punch priority order is load-bearing, and testing is what set it.** A rival monster is checked in `Monster.resolvePunch`'s street-first band immediately **after** free-roaming humans and before street enemies. The first version checked monsters first, which reads sensibly in isolation and is wrong in play: two players racing for the same civilian are *by definition* within a punch's reach of each other, so every punch landed on the rival and the contested food became unreachable for both — §8.2's "whoever grabs it gets it" cancelling itself out at exactly the moment it matters. Food-first also costs the puncher nothing, since it heals either way and the rival is still standing there next swing. **[INFERRED — `13-inferred-items.md #8(c)`.]**
- **Competitive pickups needed no new mechanism** — "first to grab wins" already fell out of the per-entity `alive` guards in `Pickup.consume`/`Human.consume` and the `HELD` exclusion in `Game.humanTargetFor`. This session's job was to *verify* it rather than build it, which it does both ways: the first punch eats and scores, the second punch on the same civilian or the same window cell gets nothing and falls through to wall damage.
- **Join in progress** (§8.1) — `Game.tryJoinPlayers`/`joinPlayer`/`pickMonsterTypeFor`, wired into `PLAY` and `CHARACTER_SELECT`. Any slot not yet in the run buys in with its own start keys: mid-day that means a monster on the street immediately at full energy with a score of zero; at the select screen it means a cursor and a pick like everybody else. The join check runs **after** the select screen's pick loop, never before — for every slot the join keys and the confirm keys are the same physical keys, so joining first would let one press both join and confirm and skip the pick. `pickMonsterTypeFor` takes the slot's default type if free and otherwise the first unused one, which matters mechanically rather than cosmetically: §2.3 keys each monster's designated victim off its type. **[INFERRED — `13-inferred-items.md #49`]**, including the deliberate exclusion: a player who is `outOfPlay` cannot use a start press as a second continue, because §7.4's window is the documented route back and Session 9 decided a closed window means out for the run.
- **`GameState.TITLE` gained an `enter` hook** that resets the roster to `activeAtStart`. Without it, a slot that joined in progress during one run would still be joined when the next run's character select opened — quietly turning `activeAtStart` into "at least" rather than "exactly".
- **`Game.spawnXFor(player)`** — one seat-position helper replacing the same arithmetic in `spawnMonsters` and `continuePlayer` (and now `joinPlayer`). Spacing is by seat *among players actually in play*, so two monsters never spawn on top of each other and a knocked-out player doesn't leave a gap in the line.
- **Three more single-player assumptions found and fixed properly, not special-cased:** the debug readout printed `ofType(Monster)[0]` — now one line per live monster, sorted by player slot, labelled with the player; `updateCrossingTimer` (Session 1's frame-rate check) took the same "first monster in the list", which silently meant P1 right up until there was a second one — now explicitly `monsterFor(0)`; and `drawHudStrip` picked a player's monster without checking `alive`, which would flash an empty energy bar over a healthy monster for one frame after a continue or a cannibalization, since the despawned one sits in the entity list until the end-of-tick sweep.
- **UI now reads its own key maps instead of naming P1's keys.** The title's "PRESS START" line is built from `CONFIG.players.slots`, the character-select hint is worded per-action ("LEFT / RIGHT TO CHOOSE — PUNCH OR JUMP TO CONFIRM") rather than naming F and G, and a new shared `keyLabel()` renders a `KeyboardEvent.code` printably for both the reserved HUD column's new `JOIN: <key>` hint and §7.4's continue prompt, so the two can't disagree about what a player's keys are called. An unjoined column still reserves exactly the space it always did.
- **Debug readout** gained a `ROSTER` line (who is in the run, on which monster, out-of-play markers, and which day a mid-day joiner bought in on) and now lists P3's keys alongside P1's and P2's.

Session 9 — the day system, progression and level features, all inside `index.html`:

- **`CONFIG.levels`** (§7) — the whole day system's *content*, and the one place a city is described: `daysPerPass: 128` and `passes: 6` (both **[FAITHFUL]**), the interstitial hold times, the headline pool, a `defaults` block, and `days`, a plain data array of authored rows. A row is `{ day, city, buildings, features, enemies, difficulty }` and every field except `day` is optional — anything omitted falls back to `defaults` field by field. **Adding a city is one row and no code change**, which is a tested claim, not an aspiration: a test pushes a brand-new row onto `CONFIG.levels.days` at runtime and then plays that day end to end.
- **`LevelData`** (a new section mirroring `06-levels-progression.md`) — the *resolver*, deliberately holding no content of its own. It owns the index arithmetic (`normalize`/`dayNumber`/`passNumber`/`isMilestone` over an absolute 0..767 index that wraps at 768), the row lookup, `layoutRow` (computes each building's x and the gaps between them from the street span, centres the row, caps the gap at `CONFIG.levels.maxGap`, and clamps every building up to `CONFIG.buildings.minRows` so a bad data row can't break traversal), `spanFor` (a riverway shortens the span so nothing is ever laid into the water), and `proceduralBuildings` (a deterministic 3-to-6-building board for an un-authored day, same no-RNG hash convention as `Building.windowLit`).
- **`CONFIG.buildings.dayLayout` is gone** — the hardcoded 5-building street Sessions 2-8 played on is now day 1's `buildings` row in `CONFIG.levels.days`, byte for byte. `Game.spawnBuildings` reads `this.day.layout` and decides nothing.
- **The authored slice** (§7.2, `13-inferred-items.md #9`) — day 1 **Peoria, IL** (the confirmed arcade opener; deliberately the gentlest board — five buildings, no features, and only the Guardsmen/helicopters §5.1 says are present every day) through day 6 **Springfield, IL** (full roster), then three deliberately-**empty rows** (days 7-9: a city name and nothing else, to show the shape a row takes before anyone authors it), then day 128 **Plano, IL** — the confirmed closer and, per §7.2, the only day with just 2 buildings. Days 10-127 have no row at all and resolve procedurally. **The city names for days 2-9 are invented placeholders, not the arcade's list** — no source consulted reproduces the ordered 128, only that it starts at Peoria and ends at Plano. Flagged in `13-inferred-items.md #9`.
- **The day loop** (§7.1/§7.2) — `PLAY` watches `dayCleared()`; `DAY_CLEAR` runs the newspaper interstitial and advances the index; `DAY_INTRO` names the next city; back to `PLAY`. The three states that had been drawing a "NOT IMPLEMENTED — SESSION 9" placeholder since Session 1 are now real, and `drawStubScreen` is deleted rather than left dead. Both interstitials are skippable with a start press.
- **Headlines** — ten short **original** comedic headlines in the arcade's spirit (the GDD explicitly asks for this rather than reproducing the arcade's own copy), picked deterministically by day index.
- **Difficulty is now driven by the day**, not typed in by hand (§5.2/§7.2) — `Game.difficultyScalar()` = the day's own term (an authored override, or `(dayNumber − 1) × dayScalarStep`) + `pass × passScalarStep` + Session 7's `CONFIG.difficulty.scalar`, which survives as a **manual debug offset**. `scaledInterval`/`scaledMax`/`policeChance`/`bombChance` all read it instead of the raw CONFIG number, so §7.2's "later passes escalate (more lightning/police)" falls out of the existing formulas rather than needing new ones. Day 1 of pass 1 still computes to exactly 0 — Session 6's flat cadences, unchanged.
- **Per-day enemy sets** — `updateSpawnDirector` now gates each of its six military cadences on `this.day.enemies`, via a new `tickSpawn` helper that also freezes a disabled timer where it is (so turning a flag on can never fire a backlog of spawns at once). Day 1 fields Guardsmen and helicopters only; later cities add vehicles, then paratroopers and lightning.
- **Level features** (§7.3) — `riverway` (a water band; standing in it drains `CONFIG.health.damage.underwaterPerSecond`, the placeholder that has been sitting unused since Session 4), `bridge` (a deck across it that is dry — `Game.inWaterAt` excludes its span), `pier` (scenery), and two new `Entity` subclasses: **`Train`** (crosses the street on a cadence; §6 prices it "per hit," the only row in the table phrased that way, so a punch pays 500 every time and three hits wreck it) and **`Boater`** (drifts along the river, 750, one punch). Both are found by `Game.featureTargetFor` in the same street-first band of `resolvePunch`'s priority chain that humans and enemies already use. Neither damages a monster on contact — §6 lists them as scoring targets, not threats.
- **Mega-vitamin at each 128-day milestone** (§7.2/§6) — `Game.awardMegaVitamin` fires the instant day 128 of a pass is *cleared*, fully heals every player still in the run and pays each the documented 5,000, and puts a flourish on the interstitial. It pays through a new `ScoreSystem.awardPlayer` (the same funnel keyed on the player record instead of a live monster) because a player mid-walk-off has no monster to award through.
- **Health carries between days** — `player.energy` is stored at day end and restored on the next day's spawn. **[INFERRED — `13-inferred-items.md #45`]** and worth calling out plainly: §7 never says health persists, but the mega-vitamin's "fully restores health" reward is meaningless if every day already starts full, so persistence is the reading that makes the documented mechanic mean something. It is also the change most likely to make the game feel harder than the original if the guess is wrong.
- **The continue window** (§7.4) — the defeated monster's walk-off *is* the window. `Game.updateContinueWindows` reads the countdown off the monster's actual screen position each tick rather than running a parallel timer that could drift; any of that player's own action keys buys back in (full energy, score intact, unlimited continues); a window that closes puts that player out while the others carry on; and `Game.runOver()` ends the run only once every joined player's window has closed. A window still open when *another* player levels the last building is closed out with the day — flagged in `13-inferred-items.md #47` as a case the arcade never had to answer.
- **Debug day jumping, three routes into one method** — `CONFIG.debug.startDay` (where a fresh run opens), the `[` / `]` / `\` keys while playing, and `RAMPAGE.jumpToDay(n)` from the console, all calling `Game.jumpToDay`, which restarts the day the normal way rather than special-casing anything. The keys need a raw edge-triggered key read outside the per-player action maps, so `InputManager` gained `wasPressed(code)` (`lastPressed`, consumed by exactly one fixed step, same discipline as every other input edge). All of it is behind `CONFIG.debug.enabled` — a shipped build has no hidden day skip.
- **Debug readout** gained a day line (absolute day, slot/pass, city, procedural-or-authored, building count, features, live difficulty scalar, milestone marker) and a level-feature/continue-window line.

**The transition design, since it's the part most likely to break.** Every paired transition happens at an *identical* y on both sides — ground↔cling both at `groundY`, cling↔roof both at `building.top`. Roof→cling routes through `attachToFace` because a roof lets the centre reach the very edge — half a body further out than a face allows — and without the re-clamp the monster hung off the side of the wall for a tick. ## Notes for the next session

_(Anything the next session needs to know that isn't obvious from the code — deferred decisions, known rough edges, things deliberately left unfinished.)_

- **`window.RAMPAGE`** exposes `{ CONFIG, game, GameState, Entity, EntityManager, Monster, InputManager, Stance, Building, CellState, BuildingState, DustPuff, cellStateForHp, Pickup }` for console poking and for the headless harness. Keep it exported — the headless tests drive the game entirely through it.
- **Update order matters now.** Buildings are added to the entity list before monsters, so a building's `update` (and therefore its collapse) runs before any monster's in the same tick. That's what makes the last tick of the SHAKING window unescapable, and it means `Building.collapse` can safely reach into monsters and change their stance — nothing has moved yet that tick. Don't reorder `spawnBuildings` / `spawnMonsters` without thinking about this. Pickups are added to the entity list dynamically (whenever a punch opens a cell), always after both, so draw order puts item icons on top.
- **Traversal gates on `building.standing`, not `building.alive`.** A rubble building is still an `alive` entity (it draws the mound). Anything new that asks "can I be on this building?" must use `standing`. `Pickup.update` uses the same flag to know when to despawn.
- **The four-stance handlers each own their exit conditions**, plus the new `DEFEATED` stance which sits outside that graph entirely — nothing transitions *into* it except `Monster.beginDefeat()` (called from `applyDamage`), and nothing transitions *out* of it (Session 9's continue flow is what eventually will). The `!b.standing → detach` safety net in the CLING/ROOF handlers is unrelated and still there for the building-collapse case.
- **A cell revealing an item doesn't stop it from being punched — it changes what punching it does.** `Building.pickupAt(col, row)` is the thing to check before assuming a punch on a HOLE cell will progress it; see `Monster.resolvePunch`. Session 5's civilians are a separate system (`Human`, not `Pickup`) but will want the same "check before you punch/grab" shape.
- **`CONFIG.buildings.dayLayout` has a height constraint**, documented in `CONFIG`: every building must be taller than the jump apex. If Session 9's `LevelData` ever authors a short building, a monster will be able to jump from the street onto its roof, which breaks the street-as-a-lane model. Either keep the constraint or decide deliberately to model the lane some other way.
- **Rooftop walking uses `walkSpeed`, climbing uses `climbSpeed`, walking off defeated uses `CONFIG.health.walkOffSpeed`** — three different speeds for three different kinds of "walking", all guesses.
- **`getPunchBox()` and `punchTargetRect()` are different boxes on purpose.** The first is the fist the art draws; the second is what damages cells and reaches further back. Debug draws both (yellow / red). **Session 5 decided this for humans**: free-roaming (street) humans are tested against `getPunchBox()`, while a human anchored to a building cell is found the same way a Pickup is (via `punchTargetRect()`/`cellTargetFor`). Sessions 6 and 10 still need to decide which box hits enemies and other monsters — probably `getPunchBox()`, same reasoning.
- **`Monster.resolvePunch` checks free-roaming humans *before* the building, not after.** This was a real finding, not an arbitrary choice: every gap in `CONFIG.buildings.dayLayout` is narrower than a fully-extended punch's reach, so if the building were checked first a street civilian would almost never be reachable. Anything Session 6/7 adds that can also stand on the open street (a street Guardsman, a vehicle) should check this ordering before assuming "check the building first" is safe — it generally isn't, on this street layout.
- **`Human` is a third thing (after `Building`/`Pickup`) that gets added to the entity list dynamically, mid-day** — window humans when a punch opens a cell (`Building.spawnOccupant`), street humans on `Game.updateHumanSpawning`'s timer. Both go through `game.entities.add`, same as `Pickup`, so draw order still puts them on top of buildings/monsters. `window.RAMPAGE` now also exports `Human`, `HumanRole`, `HumanState` for console poking and the headless harness.
- **A monster can only ever hold one human at a time** (`Monster.holding`, checked in `interactWithHuman`) — punching a *second* designated victim while already holding one just eats it instead of swapping holds. Not explicitly specified, but the simplest reading of "the monster holds its designated victim" (singular).
- **Debug readout** at the bottom of the screen (`CONFIG.debug.enabled`) is Session 1/3/4 scaffolding: small per-monster health bars, state/FPS/monster telemetry (now including energy and defeat status), the `CROSS` stopwatch, per-building structure %/state, and the collapse-attribution list. Session 8 replaces all of it with the real HUD.
- **Jump apex is ~40 px against a 46 px monster** — under one body height. It's enough to clear every gap in the current layout. Raising `CONFIG.monster.jumpSpeed` is still the right lever if jumps feel bad, but it now has a second consequence: it raises the minimum legal building height (see the point above).
- **`DAY_CLEAR` and `GAME_OVER` stubs have no `update`** — they're intentional dead ends until Session 9. Nothing transitions into them yet, including a fully-defeated solo player (there's no continue prompt or game-over trigger — the monster just despawns and `game.entities.ofType(Monster)` goes empty). `DAY_INTRO` passes straight through to `PLAY`. **`Game.dayCleared()` now returns a real answer** — you can flatten the city and nothing happens.
- **`Game.collapseEvents` is never drained.** It's reset per day in `startDay()` and grows by one entry per collapse (max five a day at the moment). Session 8 should decide whether the ScoreSystem consumes it or just reads it.
- **No spatial partitioning, no camera, no audio, no `LevelData`** — deliberately not built, since no Phase-1 mechanic needs them yet. `Game.faceTargetFor` / `roofLandingAt` / `cellTargetFor` are linear scans over five buildings; that's the whole spatial system and it's enough.

- **`window.RAMPAGE` now also exports `Enemy`, `Guardsman`, `Helicopter`, `Projectile`, `Vehicle`, `Paratrooper`, `LightningCloud`** for console poking and the headless harness.
- **A new building-cell occupant slot, `enemy`**, joins `pickup`/`human` from prior sessions — anything that adds a fourth kind of cell content (none currently planned) should extend this same three-slot pattern rather than inventing a new one.
- **`Monster.resolvePunch`'s priority chain is still five deep**: street human → street enemy → cell human → cell enemy → cell pickup → wall damage. Session 7's Vehicle/Paratrooper both turned out to be free-roaming (`Game.enemyTargetFor`), not cell-anchored, exactly as predicted — a paratrooper landed on a roof is found the same box-overlap way a street Guardsman or a low helicopter already was, no new priority tier needed. `LightningCloud` is excluded from that same query via the new `punchable` flag rather than a third special-case `instanceof` check.

Now six timers deep (window/street Guardsman, helicopter, vehicle, paratrooper, lightning); all six route through `Game.scaledInterval` for the difficulty scalar.

Kept separate on purpose so one system's sequence can't shift the other's.
- **Helicopter altitudes are keyed to the *current* `CONFIG.buildings.dayLayout`, not a fixed constant** — `strafe.diveAltitudeY` (150) was picked by hand to sit just above the tallest building in the *current* hardcoded layout (the 9-row building, roof at y=146). If Session 9's `LevelData` ever authors a taller building, this needs recomputing the same way `13-inferred-items.md #15`'s jump-apex constraint already documents a similar "the layout and a CONFIG constant must agree" trap for building heights.

Anything that wants to shove a monster somewhere (rather than just changing its stance) should call `launchAir(vx, vy, duration)` instead of setting `vx`/`vy` directly on a stance transition, or `updateAir`'s input-driven air control will erase it on the very next tick, exactly as it silently did for knockOff before this session.
- **`Vehicle`/`Paratrooper` ignore screen-edge geometry differently from `Guardsman`'s street variant** — a Vehicle drives all the way across and off the far edge unconditionally (like a Helicopter's flyover), it doesn't stop or turn around; a Paratrooper never moves horizontally at all once it starts dropping (straight down onto its assigned building's x). - **`CONFIG.enemies.helicopter.behaviorMode` default changed from `'STRAFE'` to `'AUTO'`** this session. Anything (a saved test fixture, a future session) that assumes the Session 6 default should know this — `'STRAFE'`/`'BOMB'` still work identically as explicit overrides, only the *default* behavior changed, from "always strafe" to "read `Game.bombChance()`," which is 0 at `CONFIG.difficulty.scalar = 0` and therefore behaviorally identical to the old default until the scalar is actually raised.
