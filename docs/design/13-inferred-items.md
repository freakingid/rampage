## 13. Inferred items to verify (running list)

Everything Phase 1 is guessing at rather than confirming — dial these in during implementation or verify against the arcade ROM.

**Every item below carries a status as of Session 12 — see the roll-call table immediately following.** Items whose numbers or rules actually changed also carry an inline **Session 12** note in the list itself; items the roll-call marks CONFIRMED, RESOLVED or STILL OPEN are unchanged by that session and the table says why.

### Session 12 roll-call — every item's status

Session 12 was the balance-and-fidelity pass, and its job was to leave nothing on this list unmarked. Every item below carries one of four statuses. Nothing is "pending" any more; where the answer needs the arcade ROM, that is said outright.

- **TUNED** — a number this session measured against play and changed.
- **CONFIRMED** — reviewed against a measurement and deliberately kept. No longer a first guess; the measurement is recorded in the item and in the `CONFIG` comment.
- **RESOLVED** — the question is settled and is not open any more.
- **STILL OPEN** — cannot be settled from the sources we have (needs the arcade ROM), or needs a mechanic Phase 1 deliberately didn't build. Nothing here is waiting on a decision someone forgot to make.

1. **Monster mechanical parity** — treated as *identical* (faithful to 1986; the "different stats" belief is from *World Tour*/port manuals). High confidence; confirmable in MAME. (§2.1)
2. **Damage values / health-bar size / regen amounts** — not documented; placeholder in `CONFIG`. (§4.1) **Session 12 tuned the whole table against measured play** rather than against intuition. The budget it was sized to: an unskilled player — one who walks the street punching walls, punches back at whatever drives into reach, and eats only what wanders into the swing — should take roughly 150-300 damage across a day and heal 150-250 of it back, losing about half a bar and being *forced* to eat, not killed outright. Before: 300-420 damage against 60-190 healing, i.e. one to three defeats **every day**. Damage now: `fall` 30→25 (still the largest single hit in the game), `bullet` 10→5 (the chip-damage baseline, and the most frequent hit by count), `dynamiteThrown` 20→14, `bomb` 25→18, `shell` 15→12, `tankShell` 25→18, `lightning` 20→18, `underwaterPerSecond` 8→6, `monsterPunch` 8→6 (#8). Food heals up (turkey 25→30 … toast 10→12), bad items down (poison 30→20, cactus 12→10, electrical 18→14, eaten dynamite 16→14, its fuse 26→20, photographer/bather fuse 10→8). Two supply-side levers moved with them: item density (#21) and street-civilian cadence (#28). **One thing worth stating plainly because the item's title implies otherwise: there is no passive regeneration in Phase 1 and there never was.** "Regen" here means the per-item heal table — §4.1 says only "eating refills it", and nothing in any source describes a monster recovering health by waiting. That is a reading, not a measurement, and it is the reason the *supply* of food and civilians is a balance lever at all.
3. **Movement speed, jump arc, punch cadence** — not documented numerically; tune to feel. (§3, §11.4)
4. **Health-meter representation** — bar vs. descriptive labels ("Excellent/Groggy/…"); labels confirmed for *World Tour* only. Phase 1: bar. (§4.1, §9)
5. **Building collapse threshold** — exact trigger undocumented; using per-cell HP + bottom-row/critical-mass rule. (§3.3) **Session 3 picked the numbers.** `CONFIG.buildings`: `collapseStructureFraction = 0.5` (half the building's total cell HP gone brings it down), `collapseOnBottomRowGone = true` (every bottom-row cell GONE brings it down regardless of the rest), `shakeDuration = 1.2 s` for the SHAKING window, and `roofDropStructureFraction = 0.8` for §3.2's roof-drop. Consequences on the current day layout: the bottom-row route costs 9–15 connecting punches and is deliberately the fastest — it is the same "loss of lower support" the Guard's demolition charges exploit in §3.2 — the roof-drop route costs 12–18 punches plus a landing, and punching a building apart anywhere costs 30–45. **Session 12 reviewed all four and changed none of them — it measured them instead.** The measured costs on day 1's 3-wide × 7-tall building are: bottom-row route **18** connecting punches, punch-it-apart route **32**, roof-drop **13 punches plus a landing**. Two corrections to the estimates above: the bottom-row route costs 18 and not 9-15, because a monster punching from the street hits the *second* row up first (#17), so that route clears two rows rather than one; and the roof-drop is the cheapest in punches but the most expensive in positioning, since only a landing triggers it (walking onto a roof off a face is safe), so it cannot fire by accident on the way up. The 1.2 s shake window was measured escapable at every point in it, including with only 0.05 s left — only a player who doesn't react at all takes the fall damage, which is what "the shaking state is the tell" ought to mean. At these values a 3-6 building day comes down in 25-60 s, which is an arcade day, so all four are kept. Also inferred within this: **`cellHp` (3) is spread evenly across the three non-GONE damage states**, so one punch = one state at the current value and the constant stays free to change.
6. **Monster palettes/exact colors** — sources conflict (esp. George); pick distinct palettes, treat exact hues as polish. (§10)
7. **"Holding designated victim → only Guardsmen stop firing"** — from a single walkthrough; implement as the rule, verify. (§2.3) **Session 5 built the hook**: `Monster.holding` / `Monster.isHoldingVictim` is true for exactly as long as a monster is holding its designated victim. **Session 6 wired it up and decided the scope**: a window Guardsman checks `isHoldingVictim` on the *specific monster it's about to fire at* (`Game.nearestMonster`'s result), not a global "nobody fires anywhere" switch — a Guardsman shooting at one monster has no reason to care what a different monster three buildings over is holding. Not confirmed against the arcade; the single-source claim only says "the Guardsmen," not whether that's global or per-target.
8. **Monster-vs-monster: "punch to force drop", and where a rival sits in the punch priority chain** — implemented as drop-on-hit; verify exact original behavior. (§8.2) **Session 10 built it and had to guess at three things.** (a) *The drop itself.* §8.2 flags "punching a monster can make it drop what it's holding / interrupt a grab" as inferred in the design doc's own text and instructs implementing drop-on-hit as the faithful-feeling default, which is what `Monster.interactWithMonster` does — by calling the *existing* `Human.struggleFree()` rather than adding a second release path, so a punched-out victim behaves exactly like one whose hold timer expired (#29): it lands on the street, flees, and latches `grabbable = false`, eatable by either player and re-grabbable by neither. Nothing is clawed back from the holder's score, since §6's hold bonus is paid at the moment of the grab (#41). "Interrupt a grab" is read as the same rule stated twice, not a second mechanic: a punch does not cancel the target's in-flight punch animation. (b) *Damage only, no knockback.* §5.1 gives knockback to the tank/police shell specifically and §8.2 asks only for "real damage", so `CONFIG.health.damage.monsterPunch = 8` is applied through the ordinary `applyDamage` funnel with no shove — borrowing `applyKnockback` would be inventing a mechanic rather than implementing one. At `maxEnergy` 100 that is ~13 connecting punches to down a full-health rival, deliberately slower than any military source. (c) *Priority against everything else a punch can hit.* `Monster.resolvePunch` checks a rival monster in the street-first band, immediately **after** free-roaming humans and **before** street enemies and level features. Testing is what fixed the order: with monsters checked first, two players racing for the same civilian are by definition within a punch's reach of each other, so every punch landed on the rival and the contested food became unreachable for both — §8.2's "whoever grabs it gets it" cancelling itself out. Food-first also costs the puncher nothing (it heals either way, and the rival is still there next swing). None of the three is confirmed against the arcade. **Session 12 tuned the damage and corrected the claim attached to it.** `monsterPunch` is now 6 (~17 connecting punches, ~5 s of uninterrupted contact) rather than 8. The correction matters more than the number: Session 10 justified 8 as "deliberately slower than any military source", which was never true. Punch cadence is 3.6 swings/second, so 8 damage was ~29 damage/second against a tank's ~6 — a monster's fist was the highest-damage source in the game by a factor of five, not the lowest. At 6 it is still the fastest way to down a rival *if* you can hold them in place; the real cost of doing it is that you score nothing while you do. Measured in a three-player run where the bots stood inside each other and mashed punch, this was by far the largest damage source in the game, which is what surfaced it. (§8.2, §3.2)
9. **Full city list authoring, and what fills the gaps** — Phase 1 ships the day *system* + a first slice of authored days (Peoria opening, Plano as the 2-building day); remaining 128 cities are content to fill in. (§7.2) **Session 9 built it and had to guess at three things beyond "we didn't author them all."** (a) *The city names themselves.* Only two entries in the arcade's 128-city list are confirmed by any source consulted: Peoria IL is day 1 and Plano IL is day 128. No source reproduces the ordered list in between, so days 2-9 in `CONFIG.levels.days` (Davenport, Rock Island, Galesburg, Quincy, Springfield, Decatur, Bloomington, Champaign) are **invented placeholders** chosen to fill the opening slice — they are not a fidelity claim, and replacing them is a one-line data edit each. (b) *Per-day boards.* Which buildings, how many, how tall and which level features a given city has is undocumented for every day including Peoria and Plano; the only confirmed content facts are "2-6 buildings per day" and "Plano is the only 2-building day." (c) *What an un-authored day plays like.* Rather than leaving days 10-127 unplayable or looping day 1, `LevelData.proceduralBuildings` generates a deterministic 3-to-6-building board from the day number (same no-RNG hash convention as `Building.windowLit`), and `LevelData.placeholderCity` names it `CITY No. 042`. Deliberately never 2 buildings, so Plano's in-joke stays unique. An authored row overrides all of it field by field — adding a city is one data row and no code change, verified by test. (§7.2, §7.3)
10. **Enemy fire rates / projectile speeds / spawn cadences** — undocumented; tune. (§5.1)
11. **Movement during a punch** — the GDD says nothing about whether a monster can walk while punching. Session 1 implements: punching **roots** the monster while it's on the ground (`vx = 0` for the punch duration), but airborne momentum is kept. **Session 2 extends the same rule to the two new surfaces**: punching also pins a monster clinging to a face and one standing on a roof. Verify against the arcade. (§3.1, §3.2)
12. **Air control** — no documented rule for steering mid-jump. Session 1 uses `CONFIG.monster.airControl = 0.9` (90% of walk speed while airborne). Interacts with jump-to-grab in Session 2. (§3.1)
13. **Climb speed** — no documented rate for climbing a face. Session 2 uses `CONFIG.monster.climbSpeed = 60` px/s, slower than the 84 px/s walk. Pure feel; ~~retune in Session 12~~ — **Session 12 measured it and kept it**: 3.3 s to climb the tallest day-1 building against a 6.0 s street crossing, which reads as slower-than-walking without being a chore. (§3.1)
14. **Lateral movement while clinging** — §3.1 only says "up/down climbs it". Session 2 also lets a clinging monster traverse **sideways** along the face (`climbSideSpeed = 60` px/s), clamped so the body can never slide off the frontage. Rationale: the 8-way stick, and punching cells across a building's width (§3.2) needs it. Verify whether the arcade allowed it, and at what speed. (§3.1, §3.2)
15. **The street is a lane in front of the buildings** — the GDD never says whether a building's base blocks ground movement. Session 2 implements it as a lane: a monster walks the full street past every building and only interacts with one by climbing it. Consequence: buildings are solid only for CLING (body pinned flush to the face) and ROOF (feet on the roof line). Believed faithful — the original draws monsters in front of building bases — but it is an inference. To keep this consistent, **every building must be taller than the jump apex** so a street jump can never land on a roof; `CONFIG.buildings.dayLayout` is constrained accordingly. (§3.1)
16. **Roof-edge and detach rules** — undocumented details Session 2 had to pick: a monster leaves a roof when its **centre** passes the frontage edge (so it can stand half over the edge first); jumping off a face applies a short re-grab lockout (`grabLockout = 0.18 s`) so a jump-to-grab doesn't instantly re-attach to the wall just left; and jump-to-grab needs `grabMinOverlap = 10` px of body over the frontage. All feel values. (§3.1)
17. **Which cell a punch lands on** — new in Session 3. §3.2 says a punch "rips out that section" but never says which section. Implemented as: the punch's target rect runs from the monster's **own centre** to `punchReach` past its leading edge, and the cell hit is the still-standing cell that rect overlaps most (tie-broken toward the facing direction); already-GONE sections are skipped rather than absorbing the punch, so repeated hits chew into the section behind. Reaching back to the centre rather than starting at the leading edge is load-bearing: a clinging monster is pinned flush inside the frontage, so a purely forward reach would punch clean past the building at the outermost column and that column could never be destroyed from a face. Two side effects worth verifying against the arcade: a monster standing on the street punches the **second** row up, not the bottom row, because it is more than two storeys tall and punches at chest height (the bottom row goes next, once that one is gone); and a punch thrown from the AIR damages cells too, since no stance restriction is applied. (§3.2)
18. **Score for removing an already-holed section** — new in Session 3. §6 has exactly two punch rows, "partial break" (25) and "full break / hole" (225). The third transition, HOLE → GONE, has no row. `Building.damageCell` returns `'partial'` on the first crack, `'full'` when the hole opens, and `null` for the final removal — i.e. the literal reading, where clearing a holed section scores nothing. Session 8 owns the decision when it wires up the ScoreSystem. (§3.2, §6)
19. **Rubble is scenery** — new in Session 3. §3.3 says a collapsed building "leaves a rubble mound on the street that can still contain exposed food/items to grab", and says nothing about whether it blocks or supports anything. Phase 1 implements it as pure scenery: it cannot be climbed, cannot be landed on, and does not block ground movement — consistent with the street-as-a-lane model in #15. Session 4 hangs exposed items off it. (§3.3)
20. **Fall damage is a straight drop** — new in Session 3. §3.3 says a monster caught on a collapsing building "takes a large chunk of damage from the fall" but describes no knockback. Implemented as: detach to AIR with `vy = 0` and let gravity do the rest — no upward pop, no lateral throw. The damage value (`CONFIG.health.damage.fall`, moved from `CONFIG.monster.fallDamage` in Session 4) was a placeholder 30; **Session 12 tuned it to 25** and confirmed the kinematics unchanged. It is deliberately still the single biggest hit any source in the game deals — §3.3 calls it "a large chunk" and it is the only damage the player could have avoided entirely by reacting to a 1.2 s warning. See #2, #5. (§3.3)
21. **Item distribution and identity across revealed cells** — new in Session 4. §4.2/§4.3 list the food, bad, electrical and hazard items that exist but say nothing about how densely they're distributed through a building or which window holds which. Session 4 implements: *every* cell that opens to HOLE reveals exactly one item, picked by a deterministic round-robin (`CONFIG.pickups.spawnCycle`) keyed off the cell's own position — the same no-RNG trick as `Building.windowLit`, so a reload reveals the same items in the same windows. Both the "always something" density and the specific cycle order are guesses. Retune (probably toward sparser, RNG-free-but-less-regular) in Session 12. (§4.2, §4.3) **Session 12 did exactly that.** Measured, the every-cell density made a day a conveyor belt: ~24 cells opened and ~14 items force-fed per day, and *force*-fed is the right word, because punching an open cell is the eat action (#24) — the player has no way to decline one. `CONFIG.pickups.spawnCycle` now carries `'NONE'` entries (9 of 28); a cell that opens on one is just an open wall section, and the next punch clears it to GONE. Measured across a full day-1 board that is 33% of opened cells empty, with every category still reachable. The cycle order itself is unchanged, and it is still deterministic rather than random — the sparseness is what #21 asked for, the regularity is a deliberate keep (it is what makes a reload reproducible for the test harness).
22. **Photographer / bather have no health cost if eaten in time** — new in Session 4. §4.3 says "eat them before the flash" / "eat them fast" but doesn't say whether eating them early heals, hurts, or does nothing. Implemented as nothing — `Pickup.consume()` only applies `heal`/`damage` if the definition has one, and neither `PHOTOGRAPHER` nor `BATHER` does. Their fuse timing (2.0 s) and knock-off damage if *not* eaten in time are equally undocumented guesses. **Session 12 kept the 2.0 s fuse and tuned the damage 10 → 8**, on the reasoning that what a missed photographer/bather actually costs you is the *fall* it causes (`fuseKnockOff`), not the flash itself — double-charging for one mistake made these two the most punishing thing in a wall. (§4.3)
23. **A monster defeated mid-air or mid-building snaps to the street first** — new in Session 4. §4.1 says energy hitting zero reverts to human and walks off screen, but not what happens if the monster is airborne, clinging to a face, or standing on a roof at that instant. `Monster.beginDefeat()` detaches it and sets `y = groundY` immediately, then the walk-off proceeds from there — the simplest reading, not a simulated fall or climb-down. Also inferred in the same method: the walk-off direction is whichever screen edge is nearer at the moment of defeat, and the walk-off speed (`CONFIG.health.walkOffSpeed = 70`) is its own free value, not reused from `CONFIG.monster.walkSpeed`. (§4.1)
24. **Which punch on an open cell eats the item vs. re-damages the wall** — new in Session 4. §4.3's "because punching is fast, a greedy player will sometimes grab the wrong thing" only makes sense if punching an already-open (HOLE) cell is the eat/grab action. Implemented as: `Monster.resolvePunch` checks `Building.pickupAt` before calling `damageCell` — if an item is sitting in the targeted cell, the punch consumes it instead of damaging the cell further, so the cell stays at HOLE (not GONE) until the item is gone. A further punch on the now-empty cell proceeds to `damageCell` as normal and can finally clear it to GONE. Not confirmed against the arcade. (§3.2, §4.3)
25. **Knock-off kinematics** — new in Session 4. §4.3 says dynamite/photographer/bather "knock the monster off the building" but gives no speed or arc. `Monster.knockOff()` pops the monster into AIR with a fixed outward + upward velocity (`CONFIG.health.knockOffSpeedX/Y`), independent of the source. Also inferred: the fuse-elapsed effect only reaches a monster currently attached (CLING or ROOF) to *that* building — one that already climbed away is untouched, which is what makes "leave before it triggers" a meaningful choice. (§4.3)
26. **Items don't survive a collapse into rubble** — new in Session 4. §3.3 says a rubble mound "can still contain exposed food/items to grab," but Session 4's `Pickup.update` despawns silently the instant its building stops `standing`. Rubble holding items is deferred, not implemented. (§3.3, §4.2)
27. **Punch is the single grab/eat action for humans too** — new in Session 5. §4.3's "punching is fast, a greedy player will sometimes grab the wrong thing" already implies punching an open Pickup cell *is* the eat action (#24). Human reuses that exact rule rather than inventing a separate "swat for nothing, no heal" outcome the GDD never describes for civilians. A connecting punch on any civilian (window or street) always eats it; there's no way in Phase 1 to kill one without healing. (§2.3, §4.3)
28. **Street civilians spawn already fleeing, with no proximity/AI rule** — new in Session 5. 00-core-loop §1 says civilians "flee on the street" but nothing about what triggers the flight or how densely they appear. Implemented as: `Game.updateHumanSpawning` spawns one at a screen edge every `CONFIG.humans.street.spawnInterval` (capped at `maxActive` concurrent), already in full flight toward the opposite edge, despawning once fully offscreen — no detection of a nearby monster, no idle/wander state first. 35. **Helicopter: dive/cruise altitudes, destructibility window, difficulty flag, and bomb-vs-building kinematics** — new in Session 6. §5.1 says "flies over, turns, and dives to strafe," "drops bombs from altitude," and "can be destroyed on its approach," but gives no numbers or precise rules for any of it. Implemented: `cruiseAltitudeY` is picked above every building's roofline (so the flyover never visually clips a rooftop); `strafe.diveAltitudeY` is picked just above the *tallest* building in the current day layout, so only a monster standing on that specific roof can ever reach up and punch it during its low pass — everywhere else (cruise, bombing-run altitude) it's simply unreachable, which is the "destroyed on its approach" skill window. `CONFIG.enemies.helicopter.behaviorMode` ('STRAFE'/'BOMB') is the difficulty flag SpawnDirector reads per new spawn; Session 9's day/level system is what eventually flips it automatically per §5.2's escalation curve. A bomb reaching a still-standing building's roofline damages whatever cell is exposed there and falls straight down with no horizontal carry from the helicopter's own motion (same "simplest kinematics" reasoning as knock-off, #25). (§5.1, §5.2)

    **Session 12 found the destructibility window was never actually open, and fixed it.** Session 6 picked `cruiseAltitudeY: 70` and `strafe.diveAltitudeY: 150` by hand against the single hardcoded 9-row day that existed at the time, and asserted the dive line put the helicopter within a rooftop monster's reach. It did not. A monster standing on that 9-row roof (y=146) punches in a band from y=110 to y=132; a helicopter at y=150 occupies y=136 to y=150. The two never overlapped, on *any* day — measured directly by parking a monster on the tallest roof, holding a strafing helicopter over it and swinging: unreachable on every day tested. §5.1's "can be destroyed on its approach" had therefore never been true in Phase 1, and the helicopter was the only enemy in the game that could not be destroyed at all.

    Session 9 had also quietly made it worse in a second way: once `LevelData` made the skyline per-day, a fixed 150 sat 60+ px above every roof in a city whose tallest building is five or six rows, so even the geometry the comment described stopped holding.

    Both are now resolved by a new module-level `Sky` singleton (same shape, and the same reasoning, as the `SFX` singleton — the three flying classes read altitudes from constructors and update loops with no `game` reference to thread a value through). `Game.startDay` calls `Sky.resolveFor(buildings)` once, after the layout exists and before anything flies: the cruise lane is the lower of the configured ceiling and "clear of the tallest roof by `cruiseClearance`", the dive line sits `diveAboveRoof` (16 px) *above* the tallest roof — inside a standing monster's punch box, which is the whole point — and the lightning cloud stays above the cruise lane. Day 1 is unchanged at cruise 70; its dive line moves 150 → 130. Verified by landing an actual punch on a strafing helicopter on eight different days, including procedural ones with a different tallest roof. The clearances themselves are [INFERRED], but "the strafing helicopter must be punchable from the tallest roof in the city" is now a tested property rather than a comment.

37. **Tank/police shell knockback: kinematics, direction, and the discovery that it needed new plumbing to actually travel anywhere** — new in Session 7. §5.1 says a tank's heavy shell has "large knockback (can knock a monster off a building or across the screen)" but gives no speed. `Monster.applyKnockback(fromX, speedX, speedY)` pushes the monster away from wherever the shell was fired from (not away from the building, the way the pre-existing `knockOff` does) — that's what lets a shell fired from the far side of a building still blow the monster clear of it. Both `knockOff` and `applyKnockback` now share one `Monster.launchAir(vx, vy, duration)` helper, and that consolidation surfaced a real bug rather than just being a tidy refactor: `Monster.updateAir` recomputes `vx` from the player's held direction *every tick*, which was silently overwriting a knockback's horizontal component on the very next tick — before it ever moved the monster anywhere. Session 4/5/6's photographer/bather/dynamite knock-off tests never caught this because they only checked "stance became AIR and damage applied," never travel distance. `launchAir` now arms `Monster.knockbackTimer` (`CONFIG.health.knockback.duration`, 0.35s [INFERRED]), and `updateAir` leaves `vx` alone while it's counting down instead of recomputing it from input — so this session's fix also quietly corrects the existing dynamite/photographer/bather knock-off's travel distance, which was previously near-zero regardless of `CONFIG.health.knockOffSpeedX`. (§4.3, §5.1)

43. **Day-index-driven difficulty: the shape of the ramp, and the size of a pass's step** — new in Session 9. §5.2 names five escalation axes and §7.2 says "later passes escalate (more lightning/police)", but neither gives a rate for anything. Session 7's manual `CONFIG.difficulty.scalar` is now an *offset* on top of a computed value: `Game.difficultyScalar() = day.difficulty + pass × passScalarStep + manual offset`, where `day.difficulty` is an authored row's explicit override or, failing that, `(dayNumber − 1) × dayScalarStep`. Both rates are invented: `dayScalarStep = 0.06` (so a pass ramps 0 → 7.62 across its 128 days) and `passScalarStep = 2` (so each completed pass raises every concurrent-unit cap and the police share of ground-vehicle spawns — which is how §7.2's "more lightning/police" is expressed). **Session 12 kept both rates**: the scalar's *ramp* was never the problem, what it does at the top of its range was (#36, #52). Day 1 of pass 1 with no manual offset still computes to exactly 0, i.e. Session 6's original flat cadences, so nothing about the opening day changed. Measured, not just asserted: over one simulated minute of real SpawnDirector cadence, day 5 spawns 28 units, day 100 spawns 63, and day 100 of the sixth pass spawns 107, with peak concurrent lightning going 1 → 3 → 4. A monster standing in it takes `CONFIG.health.damage.underwaterPerSecond` (Session 12: 6/s, down from Session 4's placeholder 8/s — wading is a slow drain you choose rather than a hit you take, and at 6/s it is ~16 s in the water to empty a full bar with the bridge right there) continuously. 45. **Health carries between days** — new in Session 9. §7 never says whether a monster starts a new city with the energy it ended the last one on. The reasoning is that §7.2's mega-vitamin reward ("surviving to the end of each 128-day block **fully restores health**") is meaningless if every day already starts full — a documented reward has to reward something. 46. **Mega-vitamin: when it fires and who it pays** — new in Session 9. §7.2 says surviving to the end of each 128-day block fully restores health and awards 5,000 points, and says nothing about multiplayer or about the exact moment. Implemented: it fires the instant day 128 of a pass is *cleared* (in `DAY_CLEAR`'s enter, before the day index advances), not on arriving at day 129 — "surviving to the end of the block" reads as an award for finishing it. 47. **The continue window: what a "credit" is, how many, and what a rejoining monster gets** — new in Session 9. §7.4 says a player who "re-inserts a credit before the human figure finishes walking off screen" rejoins, which the browser has no literal equivalent for. Implemented: the walk-off *is* the window (`Game.updateContinueWindows` polls it every tick from the defeated monster's own screen position, so the countdown shown is the real remaining walk-off time, not a separate timer that could drift); any of that player's own action keys buys back in; continues are **unlimited** (the arcade's limit was the player's wallet, not the machine); the rejoining monster comes back at full energy at its normal spawn position; and the score carries over untouched, since an arcade continue never wiped a score. A window that closes without input puts that player `outOfPlay` for the rest of the run while the others carry on (§7.4's "remaining monsters continue"), and the run only ends once every joined player's window has closed. One case the arcade never had to answer: another player levelling the last building *during* a walk-off. Implemented as the window ending with the day (`Game.closeContinueWindows`) — the window is the walk-off, and the walk-off is over; carrying it into the next day would make it a different mechanic. (§7.4)

    - **Hold/eat/hit-react** — a held victim gets one arm reaching toward it (`Monster.holding`); a connecting eat/grab/cannibalize opens the mouth for `CONFIG.art.eatDuration` (0.35s); a connecting hit flashes the sprite white and jolts it opposite its facing for `CONFIG.art.hitReactDuration` (0.18s). None of the three is documented at all — §10 lists the animation states the GDD wants ("grab/hold, eat, hit-react") without describing what any of them look like.
    - **Buildings** — a HOLE cell gets a jagged inner edge and a couple of debris chips (`CONFIG.buildings.debrisColor`); a CRACKED cell gets a second hairline crack and a corner chip; the rubble mound gets scorch flecks and loose debris scattered across it (`scorchColor`). §10 only says "damage states per cell" and "a rubble mound" — the texture on top is invented.
    - **Pickups/enemies** — `drawPickupIcon` gives each Pickup category its own shape (a circle+shine for food, a spiky square for bad items, a fuse-stick for dynamite, category-specific glyphs for the three weapon items, etc.) instead of a flat color square; electrical items additionally pulse with a soft glow — the "on" half of §10's "on/off variants for electrical items." The "off" (safe-to-grab) variant is a *different, unbuilt* Pickup category (item #42 — Sessions 4/6 never implemented it), so this session's glow only makes the existing lit/dangerous state read as unmistakably live; it does not add the off-state mechanic. Guardsman/Paratrooper gained a rifle line, Helicopter a spinning-blur main+tail rotor and a cockpit bubble, Vehicle tank treads/a directional barrel/(police) a flashing lightbar, and LightningCloud a three-lobe cloud silhouette in place of its rectangle.
    - **HUD** — a bordered panel per joined column (border color = that player's monster's head color), a segmented (chunky-block) energy bar that flashes when critical, and a marquee bulb row + double-line frame around the title screen's wordmark. 08-hud-ui.md's own §13 note already calls the exact arcade HUD art "inferred, Phase 1 uses clean, readable placeholders" — this session's chrome is still a placeholder, just a more arcade-styled one, and no HUD *information* (columns, what's shown per column) changed from Session 8.
    (§10, §2.1)

51. **Which of the seven documented SFX fires on which game event** — new in Session 11. §10 names seven synthesized sounds (roar, punch, collapse, explosion, eat, zap, day-clear sting) and nothing about triggers — not even that a monster's roar is tied to any specific action. Implemented, all via a module-level `SFX` singleton (`AudioManager`, WebAudio oscillator/noise synthesis, no audio files) rather than threading `game` through the many methods that need to fire one:
    - **roar** — a monster spawning/continuing (`Monster` constructor), grabbing its designated victim (`grabHuman`), and a building collapse it caused (`Building.collapse`, only when `causedBy` is set — a third-party collapse stays silent).
    - **punch** — every punch swing thrown (`Monster.update`, on the press, connecting or not), a bullet hitting a monster, a knocked-loose weapon item connecting with nothing eaten (i.e. it was dislodged, not consumed), and any enemy destroyed without healing the monster (street Guardsman, Paratrooper).
    - **collapse** — every building collapse, player-caused or not (paired with roar only for the player-caused case).
    - **explosion** — dynamite/bomb detonation, a demolition charge detonating, a tank/police shell connecting, a dropped environmental weapon destroying a vehicle/helicopter, and any Vehicle/Helicopter destroyed by a punch (read as "military hardware going up," distinct from a plain soldier being punched down).
    - **eat** — anything that heals the monster on a connecting punch: food/bad/neutral Pickups, civilians and designated victims, a window Guardsman, and cannibalizing a defeated rival's human form.
    - **zap** — anything electrical or "shock"-flavored: eating a live-electrical or bad Pickup, a lightning bolt hit, and the photographer's flash / bather's splash triggering unconsumed (the closest of the seven to "startled by a shock," since neither has its own dedicated sound).
    - **day-clear sting** — entering `DAY_CLEAR` (the newspaper interstitial), once per day, not per milestone.
    None of this mapping is sourced — it's this session's own reading of a bare SFX list, chosen for what reads as sensible reuse across ~30 call sites rather than inventing more than seven sounds. `CONFIG.audio.muteKey` ('M') toggles `SFX.muted`; the AudioContext itself unlocks on the page's first real keydown (`InputManager.attach`), not at page load, since it never runs from inside a user gesture otherwise. (§10)

52. **Bounding the escalation curve — the ramp needed a ceiling, not just a rate** — new in Session 12. §5.2 gives five escalation axes and no rates; §7.2 says later passes escalate and gives no rate either. #36/#43 invented the rates, but nothing bounded them, and over 768 days unbounded is a long way: at the top of the range (day 128 of the sixth pass, scalar 17.62) the old formulas shrank every spawn cadence to 27% of base and raised every concurrent-unit cap by +8. Measured at day 300, that meant 43 police shells landing on an unskilled player in a single day, five to eight defeats per day, and — because the street-Guardsman cap scaled too — the Guard's own demolition charges levelling most of the board while the player was busy dying. That is not a reading of "later loops escalate"; it is an unplayable tail nobody chose.

    Two new clamps, both **[INFERRED]** and neither hinted at by any source: `CONFIG.difficulty.minCadenceFactor` (0.5 — no spawn cadence ever runs faster than twice its base rate) and `maxUnitBonus` (2 — no unit category ever exceeds base + 2 concurrent). The hardest day in the game is therefore roughly "half the intervals and two more of everything than day 1", which the scalar reaches around the fourth pass and then holds. Everything about the *direction* of §5.2 survives: day 1 pass 1 is still exactly the unscaled identity, difficulty still rises with the day number and again with each pass, helicopters still shift from strafing to bombing, and ground spawns still shift from tank toward police — those last two are fractions clamped to 1 and were always bounded.

    The judgement being recorded here, so it isn't mistaken for a fidelity claim: the arcade's own difficulty curve past a few hundred days is not documented anywhere we have, and it is entirely possible the real machine did become brutally unfair that deep. Phase 1 chooses playable over speculatively faithful, and says so. (§5.2, §7.2)

## Not arcade inferences — port decisions

These aren't guesses about the 1986 original; they're choices the original had no equivalent for. Recorded so they aren't mistaken for fidelity claims.

- **Keyboard mapping** — the arcade used an 8-way joystick + Punch + Jump per player. Phase 1 maps P1 to WASD/F/G, P2 to arrows/`.`/`/`, P3 to IJKL/O/P (`CONFIG.players.slots`, Session 1).
- **Playfield resolution** — 512×384 logical px, integer-upscaled to the window. MCR-3 hardware was 512×480; the shorter playfield is a windowing choice, not a fidelity claim. (`CONFIG.view`, Session 1)
