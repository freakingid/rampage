## 13. Inferred items to verify (running list)

Everything Phase 1 is guessing at rather than confirming — dial these in during implementation or verify against the arcade ROM:

1. **Monster mechanical parity** — treated as *identical* (faithful to 1986; the "different stats" belief is from *World Tour*/port manuals). High confidence; confirmable in MAME. (§2.1)
2. **Damage values / health-bar size / regen amounts** — not documented; placeholder in `CONFIG`. (§4.1)
3. **Movement speed, jump arc, punch cadence** — not documented numerically; tune to feel. (§3, §11.4)
4. **Health-meter representation** — bar vs. descriptive labels ("Excellent/Groggy/…"); labels confirmed for *World Tour* only. Phase 1: bar. (§4.1, §9)
5. **Building collapse threshold** — exact trigger undocumented; using per-cell HP + bottom-row/critical-mass rule. (§3.3) **Session 3 picked the numbers.** `CONFIG.buildings`: `collapseStructureFraction = 0.5` (half the building's total cell HP gone brings it down), `collapseOnBottomRowGone = true` (every bottom-row cell GONE brings it down regardless of the rest), `shakeDuration = 1.2 s` for the SHAKING window, and `roofDropStructureFraction = 0.8` for §3.2's roof-drop. Consequences on the current day layout: the bottom-row route costs 9–15 connecting punches and is deliberately the fastest — it is the same "loss of lower support" the Guard's demolition charges exploit in §3.2 — the roof-drop route costs 12–18 punches plus a landing, and punching a building apart anywhere costs 30–45. All four numbers are judgement calls; Session 12 retunes them. Also inferred within this: **`cellHp` (3) is spread evenly across the three non-GONE damage states**, so one punch = one state at the current value and the constant stays free to change.
6. **Monster palettes/exact colors** — sources conflict (esp. George); pick distinct palettes, treat exact hues as polish. (§10)
7. **"Holding designated victim → only Guardsmen stop firing"** — from a single walkthrough; implement as the rule, verify. (§2.3) **Session 5 built the hook**: `Monster.holding` / `Monster.isHoldingVictim` is true for exactly as long as a monster is holding its designated victim. **Session 6 wired it up and decided the scope**: a window Guardsman checks `isHoldingVictim` on the *specific monster it's about to fire at* (`Game.nearestMonster`'s result), not a global "nobody fires anywhere" switch — a Guardsman shooting at one monster has no reason to care what a different monster three buildings over is holding. Not confirmed against the arcade; the single-source claim only says "the Guardsmen," not whether that's global or per-target.
8. **Monster-vs-monster "punch to force drop"** — implemented as drop-on-hit; verify exact original behavior. (§8.2)
9. **Full city list authoring, and what fills the gaps** — Phase 1 ships the day *system* + a first slice of authored days (Peoria opening, Plano as the 2-building day); remaining 128 cities are content to fill in. (§7.2) **Session 9 built it and had to guess at three things beyond "we didn't author them all."** (a) *The city names themselves.* Only two entries in the arcade's 128-city list are confirmed by any source consulted: Peoria IL is day 1 and Plano IL is day 128. No source reproduces the ordered list in between, so days 2-9 in `CONFIG.levels.days` (Davenport, Rock Island, Galesburg, Quincy, Springfield, Decatur, Bloomington, Champaign) are **invented placeholders** chosen to fill the opening slice — they are not a fidelity claim, and replacing them is a one-line data edit each. (b) *Per-day boards.* Which buildings, how many, how tall and which level features a given city has is undocumented for every day including Peoria and Plano; the only confirmed content facts are "2-6 buildings per day" and "Plano is the only 2-building day." (c) *What an un-authored day plays like.* Rather than leaving days 10-127 unplayable or looping day 1, `LevelData.proceduralBuildings` generates a deterministic 3-to-6-building board from the day number (same no-RNG hash convention as `Building.windowLit`), and `LevelData.placeholderCity` names it `CITY No. 042`. Deliberately never 2 buildings, so Plano's in-joke stays unique. An authored row overrides all of it field by field — adding a city is one data row and no code change, verified by test. (§7.2, §7.3)
10. **Enemy fire rates / projectile speeds / spawn cadences** — undocumented; tune. (§5.1)
11. **Movement during a punch** — the GDD says nothing about whether a monster can walk while punching. Session 1 implements: punching **roots** the monster while it's on the ground (`vx = 0` for the punch duration), but airborne momentum is kept. **Session 2 extends the same rule to the two new surfaces**: punching also pins a monster clinging to a face and one standing on a roof. Verify against the arcade. (§3.1, §3.2)
12. **Air control** — no documented rule for steering mid-jump. Session 1 uses `CONFIG.monster.airControl = 0.9` (90% of walk speed while airborne). Interacts with jump-to-grab in Session 2. (§3.1)
13. **Climb speed** — no documented rate for climbing a face. Session 2 uses `CONFIG.monster.climbSpeed = 60` px/s, slower than the 84 px/s walk. Pure feel; retune in Session 12. (§3.1)
14. **Lateral movement while clinging** — §3.1 only says "up/down climbs it". Session 2 also lets a clinging monster traverse **sideways** along the face (`climbSideSpeed = 60` px/s), clamped so the body can never slide off the frontage. Rationale: the 8-way stick, and punching cells across a building's width (§3.2) needs it. Verify whether the arcade allowed it, and at what speed. (§3.1, §3.2)
15. **The street is a lane in front of the buildings** — the GDD never says whether a building's base blocks ground movement. Session 2 implements it as a lane: a monster walks the full street past every building and only interacts with one by climbing it. Consequence: buildings are solid only for CLING (body pinned flush to the face) and ROOF (feet on the roof line). Believed faithful — the original draws monsters in front of building bases — but it is an inference. To keep this consistent, **every building must be taller than the jump apex** so a street jump can never land on a roof; `CONFIG.buildings.dayLayout` is constrained accordingly. (§3.1)
16. **Roof-edge and detach rules** — undocumented details Session 2 had to pick: a monster leaves a roof when its **centre** passes the frontage edge (so it can stand half over the edge first); jumping off a face applies a short re-grab lockout (`grabLockout = 0.18 s`) so a jump-to-grab doesn't instantly re-attach to the wall just left; and jump-to-grab needs `grabMinOverlap = 10` px of body over the frontage. All feel values. (§3.1)
17. **Which cell a punch lands on** — new in Session 3. §3.2 says a punch "rips out that section" but never says which section. Implemented as: the punch's target rect runs from the monster's **own centre** to `punchReach` past its leading edge, and the cell hit is the still-standing cell that rect overlaps most (tie-broken toward the facing direction); already-GONE sections are skipped rather than absorbing the punch, so repeated hits chew into the section behind. Reaching back to the centre rather than starting at the leading edge is load-bearing: a clinging monster is pinned flush inside the frontage, so a purely forward reach would punch clean past the building at the outermost column and that column could never be destroyed from a face. Two side effects worth verifying against the arcade: a monster standing on the street punches the **second** row up, not the bottom row, because it is more than two storeys tall and punches at chest height (the bottom row goes next, once that one is gone); and a punch thrown from the AIR damages cells too, since no stance restriction is applied. (§3.2)
18. **Score for removing an already-holed section** — new in Session 3. §6 has exactly two punch rows, "partial break" (25) and "full break / hole" (225). The third transition, HOLE → GONE, has no row. `Building.damageCell` returns `'partial'` on the first crack, `'full'` when the hole opens, and `null` for the final removal — i.e. the literal reading, where clearing a holed section scores nothing. Session 8 owns the decision when it wires up the ScoreSystem. (§3.2, §6)
19. **Rubble is scenery** — new in Session 3. §3.3 says a collapsed building "leaves a rubble mound on the street that can still contain exposed food/items to grab", and says nothing about whether it blocks or supports anything. Phase 1 implements it as pure scenery: it cannot be climbed, cannot be landed on, and does not block ground movement — consistent with the street-as-a-lane model in #15. Session 4 hangs exposed items off it. (§3.3)
20. **Fall damage is a straight drop** — new in Session 3. §3.3 says a monster caught on a collapsing building "takes a large chunk of damage from the fall" but describes no knockback. Implemented as: detach to AIR with `vy = 0` and let gravity do the rest — no upward pop, no lateral throw. The damage value (now `CONFIG.health.damage.fall = 30`, moved from `CONFIG.monster.fallDamage` in Session 4) is a placeholder; see #2. (§3.3)
21. **Item distribution and identity across revealed cells** — new in Session 4. §4.2/§4.3 list the food, bad, electrical and hazard items that exist but say nothing about how densely they're distributed through a building or which window holds which. Session 4 implements: *every* cell that opens to HOLE reveals exactly one item, picked by a deterministic round-robin (`CONFIG.pickups.spawnCycle`) keyed off the cell's own position — the same no-RNG trick as `Building.windowLit`, so a reload reveals the same items in the same windows. Both the "always something" density and the specific cycle order are guesses. Retune (probably toward sparser, RNG-free-but-less-regular) in Session 12. (§4.2, §4.3)
22. **Photographer / bather have no health cost if eaten in time** — new in Session 4. §4.3 says "eat them before the flash" / "eat them fast" but doesn't say whether eating them early heals, hurts, or does nothing. Implemented as nothing — `Pickup.consume()` only applies `heal`/`damage` if the definition has one, and neither `PHOTOGRAPHER` nor `BATHER` does. Their fuse timing (2.0 s) and knock-off damage (10) if *not* eaten in time are equally undocumented guesses. (§4.3)
23. **A monster defeated mid-air or mid-building snaps to the street first** — new in Session 4. §4.1 says energy hitting zero reverts to human and walks off screen, but not what happens if the monster is airborne, clinging to a face, or standing on a roof at that instant. `Monster.beginDefeat()` detaches it and sets `y = groundY` immediately, then the walk-off proceeds from there — the simplest reading, not a simulated fall or climb-down. Also inferred in the same method: the walk-off direction is whichever screen edge is nearer at the moment of defeat, and the walk-off speed (`CONFIG.health.walkOffSpeed = 70`) is its own free value, not reused from `CONFIG.monster.walkSpeed`. (§4.1)
24. **Which punch on an open cell eats the item vs. re-damages the wall** — new in Session 4. §4.3's "because punching is fast, a greedy player will sometimes grab the wrong thing" only makes sense if punching an already-open (HOLE) cell is the eat/grab action. Implemented as: `Monster.resolvePunch` checks `Building.pickupAt` before calling `damageCell` — if an item is sitting in the targeted cell, the punch consumes it instead of damaging the cell further, so the cell stays at HOLE (not GONE) until the item is gone. A further punch on the now-empty cell proceeds to `damageCell` as normal and can finally clear it to GONE. Not confirmed against the arcade. (§3.2, §4.3)
25. **Knock-off kinematics** — new in Session 4. §4.3 says dynamite/photographer/bather "knock the monster off the building" but gives no speed or arc. `Monster.knockOff()` pops the monster into AIR with a fixed outward + upward velocity (`CONFIG.health.knockOffSpeedX/Y`), independent of the source. Also inferred: the fuse-elapsed effect only reaches a monster currently attached (CLING or ROOF) to *that* building — one that already climbed away is untouched, which is what makes "leave before it triggers" a meaningful choice. (§4.3)
26. **Items don't survive a collapse into rubble** — new in Session 4. §3.3 says a rubble mound "can still contain exposed food/items to grab," but Session 4's `Pickup.update` despawns silently the instant its building stops `standing`. Rubble holding items is deferred, not implemented. (§3.3, §4.2)
27. **Punch is the single grab/eat action for humans too** — new in Session 5. §4.3's "punching is fast, a greedy player will sometimes grab the wrong thing" already implies punching an open Pickup cell *is* the eat action (#24). Human reuses that exact rule rather than inventing a separate "swat for nothing, no heal" outcome the GDD never describes for civilians. A connecting punch on any civilian (window or street) always eats it; there's no way in Phase 1 to kill one without healing. (§2.3, §4.3)
28. **Street civilians spawn already fleeing, with no proximity/AI rule** — new in Session 5. 00-core-loop §1 says civilians "flee on the street" but nothing about what triggers the flight or how densely they appear. Implemented as: `Game.updateHumanSpawning` spawns one at a screen edge every `CONFIG.humans.street.spawnInterval` (capped at `maxActive` concurrent), already in full flight toward the opposite edge, despawning once fully offscreen — no detection of a nearby monster, no idle/wander state first. The civilian heal amount (`CONFIG.humans.civilianHeal = 15`) is an equally undocumented placeholder alongside the rest of `13-inferred-items.md #2`. (§4.2, 00-core-loop §1)
29. **A struggled-free designated victim drops to the street and flees like a civilian** — new in Session 5. §2.3 says the victim "struggles free" and "can then be eaten" but not where it ends up or what it does next. `Human.struggleFree()` drops it at the monster's current position, snaps it to street level, and sends it fleeing toward the nearer screen edge (the same "flee toward the nearer edge" convention Session 4's `beginDefeat` already uses for a defeated monster's walk-off). `grabbable` latches `false` permanently, so it can only ever be eaten after this, never re-grabbed — even by its own monster. (§2.3)
30. **Hold-time range and its pseudo-random source** — new in Session 5. §2.3 says "a random hold time" with no range. `CONFIG.humans.holdTimeMin/Max` (3–6 s) is a guess. The randomness itself is a deterministic hash (`Game.nextHoldTime`, keyed off an incrementing counter) rather than `Math.random()`, matching this file's established "no RNG" convention so reloads and the headless test harness stay reproducible — same reasoning as `Building.windowLit` and `CONFIG.pickups.spawnCycle`. (§2.3)
31. **Window occupancy split between Pickup and Human** — new in Session 5. Session 4 already made every opened cell reveal exactly one Pickup (#21); Session 5 adds civilians and designated victims into windows too without a documented ratio. Implemented as a second deterministic cycle, `CONFIG.humans.windowOccupancyCycle`, keyed the same way as `CONFIG.pickups.spawnCycle` but decided independently: most entries defer to the existing Pickup cycle ('ITEM'), a minority name a Human type instead, so Session 4's item density and flavor still dominates. Both the density and the specific cycle order are guesses. (§2.3, §4.2)
32. **A street human standing in the way is hit before the wall behind it** — new in Session 5. Not documented either way, but it fell out of testing: `CONFIG.buildings.dayLayout`'s gaps between buildings are all narrower than a fully-extended punch's reach, so if wall-targeting were checked first, a street civilian would almost never be reachable — the wider wall-reaching rect would always find some building cell first. `Monster.resolvePunch` checks free-roaming street humans (via the narrower `getPunchBox()`) before it checks the building at all, which also matches the intuitive reading that someone standing between the monster and a wall takes the punch meant for them. (§2.3, §3.2)
33. **Enemy fire rates, projectile speeds, spawn cadences, and bomb damage** — new in Session 6, filling in §5.1's explicit "not documented anywhere" gap (already flagged as #10). `CONFIG.enemies` and `CONFIG.spawnDirector` carry every number: window-Guardsman fire interval/jitter/dynamite mix/target range, street-Guardsman run speed and plant time, demolition-charge fuse, helicopter cruise/dive altitudes and speeds, bullet/dynamite/bomb speeds and blast radii, and all three SpawnDirector cadences. `CONFIG.health.damage.bomb = 25` is the one new health-table entry (siblings `bullet`/`dynamiteThrown` were already placeholders from Session 4). All first-pass guesses; Session 12 retunes them. (§5.1)
34. **Guardsman: window occupancy rule, street-variant destructibility, and thrown-dynamite kinematics** — new in Session 6. Three separate guesses bundled under one Guardsman class: (a) a window Guardsman can only pop from a still-INTACT-or-CRACKED cell — §5.1 never says whether it needs a wall behind it, but a HOLE/GONE cell reads as "already blown open," so `Building.spawnEnemyWindow` requires an intact-ish cell, same reasoning as the punch-target-rect's "needs a wall" logic (#17); (b) the street variant (running to plant a demolition charge) can be destroyed by a connecting punch mid-run or mid-plant, same as the window variant, but does **not** heal the monster on destruction — §5.1 only documents "removes threat + heals" for the window row; (c) thrown dynamite is a simple ballistic lob toward wherever the target monster is standing at throw time, with no homing once airborne and no data on throw speed/arc. (§5.1)
35. **Helicopter: dive/cruise altitudes, destructibility window, difficulty flag, and bomb-vs-building kinematics** — new in Session 6. §5.1 says "flies over, turns, and dives to strafe," "drops bombs from altitude," and "can be destroyed on its approach," but gives no numbers or precise rules for any of it. Implemented: `cruiseAltitudeY` is picked above every building's roofline (so the flyover never visually clips a rooftop); `strafe.diveAltitudeY` is picked just above the *tallest* building in the current day layout, so only a monster standing on that specific roof can ever reach up and punch it during its low pass — everywhere else (cruise, bombing-run altitude) it's simply unreachable, which is the "destroyed on its approach" skill window. `CONFIG.enemies.helicopter.behaviorMode` ('STRAFE'/'BOMB') is the difficulty flag SpawnDirector reads per new spawn; Session 9's day/level system is what eventually flips it automatically per §5.2's escalation curve. A bomb reaching a still-standing building's roofline damages whatever cell is exposed there and falls straight down with no horizontal carry from the helicopter's own motion (same "simplest kinematics" reasoning as knock-off, #25). (§5.1, §5.2)
36. **Difficulty escalation scalar** — new in Session 7. §5.2 names five escalation axes but gives no rates, and axis 1 ("more buildings") can't be built at all until Session 9's `LevelData` replaces the hardcoded `dayLayout`. `CONFIG.difficulty.scalar` is a single manual knob (default 0) standing in for the real day/level system; `Game.scaledInterval`/`scaledMax` shrink SpawnDirector cadences and raise concurrent-unit caps as it rises (axis 2, and axis 4 for lightning), `Game.policeChance()` shifts new ground-vehicle spawns from tank toward police (axis 2/5), and `Game.bombChance()` shifts new helicopters from STRAFE toward BOMB when `CONFIG.enemies.helicopter.behaviorMode` is left at its new `'AUTO'` default (axis 3; `'STRAFE'`/`'BOMB'` still force a single mode manually, Session 6's original behavior). At `scalar = 0` every formula is the identity, so Session 6's flat cadences are unchanged until something (today, a manual edit; eventually Session 9's day index) raises it. Every formula's rate (`cadenceScale`, `maxUnitGrowth`, `policeShareBase/Growth`, `bombChanceGrowth`) is invented — the GDD confirms only the direction of each axis, never a number. (§5.2)
37. **Tank/police shell knockback: kinematics, direction, and the discovery that it needed new plumbing to actually travel anywhere** — new in Session 7. §5.1 says a tank's heavy shell has "large knockback (can knock a monster off a building or across the screen)" but gives no speed. `Monster.applyKnockback(fromX, speedX, speedY)` pushes the monster away from wherever the shell was fired from (not away from the building, the way the pre-existing `knockOff` does) — that's what lets a shell fired from the far side of a building still blow the monster clear of it. Both `knockOff` and `applyKnockback` now share one `Monster.launchAir(vx, vy, duration)` helper, and that consolidation surfaced a real bug rather than just being a tidy refactor: `Monster.updateAir` recomputes `vx` from the player's held direction *every tick*, which was silently overwriting a knockback's horizontal component on the very next tick — before it ever moved the monster anywhere. Session 4/5/6's photographer/bather/dynamite knock-off tests never caught this because they only checked "stance became AIR and damage applied," never travel distance. `launchAir` now arms `Monster.knockbackTimer` (`CONFIG.health.knockback.duration`, 0.35s [INFERRED]), and `updateAir` leaves `vx` alone while it's counting down instead of recomputing it from input — so this session's fix also quietly corrects the existing dynamite/photographer/bather knock-off's travel distance, which was previously near-zero regardless of `CONFIG.health.knockOffSpeedX`. (§4.3, §5.1)
38. **Tank/police: one-punch destruction, shared spawn cadence, and the Guardsman-only "holding stops fire" scope** — new in Session 7. §5.1 calls the tank "heavily armored," but nothing in Phase 1 models extra hit points for *any* enemy — Session 6's Guardsmen and helicopter are both one-connecting-punch kills too, so `Vehicle.consume()` follows the same convention rather than inventing hit points found nowhere in the GDD. Tank and police share one `Vehicle` class (`variant`), one SpawnDirector cadence and concurrent cap, and one shell projectile kind — `Game.policeChance()` (#36) picks which variant a given spawn roll is. Deliberately **not** implemented: `Monster.isHoldingVictim` gates only the Guardsmen's fire (item #7's single source names "the Guardsmen" specifically, not "the military"), so Vehicle/Paratrooper/LightningCloud all ignore a monster's held-victim status and keep firing. Speeds, fire intervals, and target range are first-pass guesses; Session 12 retunes them. (§5.1)
39. **Paratrooper drop/fire cadence, and lightning's "hazard not a unit" targeting** — new in Session 7. Paratrooper: §5.1 gives no drop speed or fire interval, only that it's "rapid" relative to a window Guardsman's — implemented as roughly 3× a window Guardsman's fire rate, no other basis. It reuses the plain bullet (no distinct paratrooper weapon is named) and is destroyed the same one-punch way as every other Session 6/7 enemy; §5.1 doesn't say whether a punch works on it before or during its drop, so the drop's geometry (descending from cruise altitude, well above any roof a monster could be standing on) is what makes it unreachable until DEFENDING — the same "unreachable until a specific moment" shape Session 6 used for the helicopter's strafing dive (#35). Lightning cloud: §5.1 explicitly calls it "a hazard rather than a unit," so it's the one `Enemy` subclass with `punchable = false` — nothing in Phase 1 can destroy it, only avoid it. It also fires positionally rather than at a target: a bolt drops straight down from wherever the cloud currently is on a timer, with no monster-seeking logic, since "drifts over the city and fires lightning bolts" reads as a hazard's schedule rather than a unit's fire-control the way a Guardsman/vehicle/paratrooper's target-seeking fire does. Drift speed, altitude, and bolt cadence are all first-pass guesses. (§5.1)
40. **Environmental weapons (manhole cover / flower pot / safe): modeled as a new Pickup category that a punch turns into a falling object instead of eating** — new in Session 7. §5.1/§6 name the three items and say they "can be knocked loose and dropped onto ground and air units," but nothing describes the actual mechanic — not even whether the monster carries and throws them, or a punch simply dislodges them where they sit. Implemented as the simplest reading that reuses existing machinery: they're `Pickup` items (`category: 'weapon'`) that reveal in a building cell exactly like food/bad items (§4.2's existing `spawnCycle`/`windowOccupancyCycle` mechanism, extended rather than replaced), but a connecting punch doesn't heal or damage the monster — `Pickup.consume` hands a weapon item off to a new `Projectile.spawnDroppedObject`, which falls straight down from the cell's position and destroys the first Tank/Police/Helicopter its bounding box reaches (`Game.groundOrAirUnitHitBy`), or vanishes harmlessly at street level otherwise. Deliberately **not** included as drop targets: Guardsmen and paratroopers, since both are already vulnerable to a direct punch and §5.1 pairs this mechanic specifically with "ground and air units," which reads as the vehicle/aircraft roster. Fall speed, one-shot-kill resolution, and the lack of any monster credit/scoring hook (Session 8's job) are all invented. (§4.3, §5.1, §6)
41. **Scoring: which moment awards the two ranged rows, and how a value inside each range is picked** — new in Session 8. §6 gives the designated-victim hold bonus and the dropped safe as ranges (4,000-6,000 and 100-500) rather than single numbers, and says nothing about when a range-valued or single-valued reward actually lands. Implemented: the hold bonus is awarded the instant `Monster.grabHuman` succeeds, not when the victim later struggles free or gets eaten — a monster punched off the building or defeated mid-hold keeps the credit either way, which reads as more natural than losing it to bad luck after the catch already happened. Both ranges resolve to one value via `ScoreSystem.pickInRange`, which reads `Game.nextScoreFrac()` — a third deterministic pseudo-random counter, same hash-off-an-incrementing-seed trick as `nextHoldTime` (#30) and `nextSpawnFrac` (#33), on its own seed so it can't perturb either. Also folded in here: eating *any* `Human` — a plain civilian, the wrong monster's designated victim, or a struggled-free former victim — scores the same flat "eat a civilian" 500, since §6 gives no separate row for the other two cases and Session 5 already treats them as "just another civilian" for healing purposes. (§2.3, §6)
42. **Nine §6 scoring rows have no code path — the underlying entity/feature was never built, not overlooked** — new in Session 8. `CONFIG.scoring` carries every row's exact documented value regardless (same "defined but unreachable" shape `CONFIG.health.damage` used in Session 4), but nothing in Sessions 1-7 ever built: a generic street "Car" in any of its three states (parked/moving/parked-then-takeoff — the Vehicle class is Tank/Police only, a different roster entirely per 04-military-hazards.md), a "bag of loot" entity, or the "off" (safe-to-grab) variant of the light bulb/TV/neon sign that 03-eating-health.md §4.3 explicitly calls for ("when off, TV/light bulb/neon can instead be knocked out for points") — Sessions 4/6 only ever implemented the lit/dangerous one, always under `category: 'electrical'`, always damaging. Boater/train and the mega-vitamin bonus were also on this list at the end of Session 8; **Session 9 closed all three** (`Train.punch`/`Boater.punch`/`Game.awardMegaVitamin`), leaving six still-unreachable rows — the three car states, the loot bag, and the three "off" electrical variants. None of them is silently dropped; each is a named `CONFIG.scoring` entry with a comment pointing at what's missing. (§4.3, §6)
43. **Day-index-driven difficulty: the shape of the ramp, and the size of a pass's step** — new in Session 9. §5.2 names five escalation axes and §7.2 says "later passes escalate (more lightning/police)", but neither gives a rate for anything. Session 7's manual `CONFIG.difficulty.scalar` is now an *offset* on top of a computed value: `Game.difficultyScalar() = day.difficulty + pass × passScalarStep + manual offset`, where `day.difficulty` is an authored row's explicit override or, failing that, `(dayNumber − 1) × dayScalarStep`. Both rates are invented: `dayScalarStep = 0.06` (so a pass ramps 0 → 7.62 across its 128 days) and `passScalarStep = 2` (so each completed pass adds +1 to every concurrent-unit cap, lightning included, and +0.16 to the police share of ground-vehicle spawns — which is how §7.2's "more lightning/police" is expressed). Day 1 of pass 1 with no manual offset still computes to exactly 0, i.e. Session 6's original flat cadences, so nothing about the opening day changed. Measured, not just asserted: over one simulated minute of real SpawnDirector cadence, day 5 spawns 28 units, day 100 spawns 63, and day 100 of the sixth pass spawns 107, with peak concurrent lightning going 1 → 3 → 4. (§5.2, §7.2)

44. **Level features: everything except "water drains health"** — new in Session 9. §7.3 names four set-pieces (bridge, riverway, piers + boater, train/trolley) and gives exactly one rule about any of them — going under the water drains health — with no geometry, speeds, or interaction rules. Everything below is the cheapest reading that makes each one a real mechanic rather than a backdrop, and none of it is confirmed:
    - **Riverway** — a band of water occupying the far end of the street. A monster standing in it takes `CONFIG.health.damage.underwaterPerSecond` (8/s, itself a Session 4 placeholder) continuously. Only from the GROUND stance: a monster clinging to a face or on a roof can't be in the water (a riverway shortens the span buildings are laid out across, so nothing stands in the river), and one mid-air over the river is over it, not in it. The side-on street means "in the river" is drawn at the same feet line as the street — a wading overlay is an art-pass concern, not a mechanic.
    - **Bridge** — a deck across part of the riverway; standing on its span is dry. §7.3 lists bridge and riverway as separate features and never connects them, but a bridge that isn't the safe way over the water isn't a mechanic at all, so they're implemented as a pair.
    - **Pier** — scenery at the near bank. It exists so the boater has somewhere to be; nothing interacts with it.
    - **Train** — crosses the street on a fixed cadence. §6 prices it "per hit" — the only row in the whole table phrased that way — so a punch pays 500 *every* time and it takes `hitsToDestroy` (3, invented) to wreck it.
    - **Boater** — drifts along the river; one punch destroys it for §6's 750. It's a person in a boat, but eating it is **not** implemented as a heal: §6 lists it as a scoring target and §4.2 never names it as food.
    - Neither the train nor the boater damages a monster on contact. Nothing in the GDD says they do, and §6 lists both as scoring rows, not as threats.
    Both are `Entity` subclasses rather than `Enemy` (they never attack) or `Human` (they aren't food), and are found by a punch through their own `Game.featureTargetFor`, in the same street-first band of `Monster.resolvePunch`'s priority chain as humans and enemies (#32). (§7.3, §6)

45. **Health carries between days** — new in Session 9. §7 never says whether a monster starts a new city with the energy it ended the last one on. Implemented as: it does — `player.energy` is written back at day end (`Game.storeEnergies`) and restored on the next day's spawn, clamped to a minimum of 1. The reasoning is that §7.2's mega-vitamin reward ("surviving to the end of each 128-day block **fully restores health**") is meaningless if every day already starts full — a documented reward has to reward something. Not confirmed against the arcade, and it is the single change most likely to make the game feel harder than the original if it's wrong. (§7.1, §7.2, §4.1)

46. **Mega-vitamin: when it fires and who it pays** — new in Session 9. §7.2 says surviving to the end of each 128-day block fully restores health and awards 5,000 points, and says nothing about multiplayer or about the exact moment. Implemented: it fires the instant day 128 of a pass is *cleared* (in `DAY_CLEAR`'s enter, before the day index advances), not on arriving at day 129 — "surviving to the end of the block" reads as an award for finishing it. Every player still in play is healed and paid; a player whose continue window closed earlier gets nothing, and one whose window is still open when the day clears is closed out first (#47) and so also gets nothing. The award goes through a new `ScoreSystem.awardPlayer` rather than the usual monster-keyed `award`, because a player mid-walk-off has no live monster to award through. (§7.2, §6)

47. **The continue window: what a "credit" is, how many, and what a rejoining monster gets** — new in Session 9. §7.4 says a player who "re-inserts a credit before the human figure finishes walking off screen" rejoins, which the browser has no literal equivalent for. Implemented: the walk-off *is* the window (`Game.updateContinueWindows` polls it every tick from the defeated monster's own screen position, so the countdown shown is the real remaining walk-off time, not a separate timer that could drift); any of that player's own action keys buys back in; continues are **unlimited** (the arcade's limit was the player's wallet, not the machine); the rejoining monster comes back at full energy at its normal spawn position; and the score carries over untouched, since an arcade continue never wiped a score. A window that closes without input puts that player `outOfPlay` for the rest of the run while the others carry on (§7.4's "remaining monsters continue"), and the run only ends once every joined player's window has closed. One case the arcade never had to answer: another player levelling the last building *during* a walk-off. Implemented as the window ending with the day (`Game.closeContinueWindows`) — the window is the walk-off, and the walk-off is over; carrying it into the next day would make it a different mechanic. (§7.4)

## Not arcade inferences — port decisions

These aren't guesses about the 1986 original; they're choices the original had no equivalent for. Recorded so they aren't mistaken for fidelity claims.

- **Keyboard mapping** — the arcade used an 8-way joystick + Punch + Jump per player. Phase 1 maps P1 to WASD/F/G, P2 to arrows/`.`/`/`, P3 to IJKL/O/P (`CONFIG.players.slots`, Session 1).
- **Playfield resolution** — 512×384 logical px, integer-upscaled to the window. MCR-3 hardware was 512×480; the shorter playfield is a windowing choice, not a fidelity claim. (`CONFIG.view`, Session 1)
